Who is doing what to whom?

Explore Cree person, number, reference and direction. These are grammatical distinctions, not interchangeable English pronouns. The documented forms below remain dialect-specific.

Person and number
  • 1 — I / me (nîtha in Woodland; nîya in Plains)
  • 2 — you, singular (kîtha / kîya)
  • 3 — she/he/they (wîtha / wîya)
  • 1p exclusive — we, not you
  • 21 inclusive — we, including you
  • 2p — you plural
  • 3p — they
Third-person reference
  • 3 proximate — a discourse-central third person
  • 3′ obviative — another third person
  • Further obviative — distinguish additional third persons where supported by the dialect and construction
  • Absentative — absent/deceased referent where appropriate
  • Near / far demonstratives — physical or discourse distance, not the same as obviation
Verb relationships
  • Direct — subject acting on object under the person hierarchy
  • Inverse — reverse relationship, not simply a passive
  • Local 1↔2 — I act on you / you act on me
  • Singular / plural actor and undergoer
  • Independent / conjunct / imperative, when attested

Not all of these categories have verified forms in the current dataset. Missing paradigms are identified rather than generated from unverified endings.
