How Cree Sentences Work

Word order · Syntax · Meaning · Sentence building

1. What is syntax?

Syntax is how words and phrases fit together to make a sentence. English often relies on word order to show who does an action. Cree can also use information contained in the verb itself to identify the people involved.

Morphology

How a word is built from meaningful parts: roots, stems, prefixes, preverbs, finals and inflections.

Syntax

How words and phrases work together in a sentence, including their order and how they relate.

Cree does not simply work like English spoken backwards. Cree sentence structure is shaped by verb morphology, person marking, animacy, obviation, and what speakers want to highlight.

4. Why Cree word order can be flexible

Person marking

Cree verb forms can mark participants such as first person (I/we) and second person (you).

Animate and inanimate classes

Grammatical noun classes help shape which verb forms are used. Animacy is grammatical, not simply a question of whether something is alive.

Direct and inverse

In transitive animate verbs, direct/inverse morphology helps express the relationship between participants according to Cree's person hierarchy. It is not just a way of swapping English subjects and objects.

Obviation

When a clause or stretch of discourse has more than one third-person participant, obviation helps distinguish them.

Topic and focus

Speakers may place material in different positions depending on what is already known, what is new, or what they want to emphasize.

Actual Woods Cree (TH) and Plains Cree (Y) sentences must be taught using examples documented for each variety. The rearranged English sentences above do not establish interchangeable Cree word orders.
