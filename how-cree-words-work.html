How Cree Words Work

Interactive lesson on possession, descriptive word parts, and verbs that describe washing. Based on Chelsea Vowel’s article Forming words in Cree (2011).

Plains Cree · Y dialect examples

This lesson follows the published examples in the linked article. These spellings are not presented as verified Lac La Ronge Woodland (TH) equivalents.

1. Whose hand?

The published example uses micihciy (“hand”). Its mi- placeholder is replaced when a possessor is specified.

2. Build a descriptive word

Choose a published descriptor to see how it combines with the -cihciy hand element.

3. Noun or verb?

Compare wîpicihciy (“dirty hand”) and wîpicihcê (“to have a dirty hand”). The article identifies their shared cihc element.

Noun
wîpicihciy

Dirty hand

Verb
wîpicihcê

To have a dirty hand

4. Washing words

Select an expression from the article to see its meaning. These are examples, not a general-purpose word constructor.

The article gives kâsîcihcê(k) / kâsîhkwê(k) as commands for washing hands/face, while other listed forms are dictionary-style descriptions. Keep these grammatical functions distinct.
