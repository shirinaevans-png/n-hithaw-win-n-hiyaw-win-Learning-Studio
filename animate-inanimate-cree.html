Animate & Inanimate Cree Words

Discover how Cree groups nouns as animate or inanimate, and how those groups affect the verbs we use. In Cree, animate and inanimate are grammar categories—not simply a question of whether something is alive.

2. How noun class affects verbs

In English, we might say that someone is strong or that something is hard. Cree often expresses descriptions like these with verbs. The form of the verb depends partly on whether the noun being described is grammatically animate or inanimate. Explore the examples below to see the difference.

5. Understanding the four verb classes

VAI · Animate intransitive

Used when the verb describes an animate subject, such as an animate noun being red.

VII · Inanimate intransitive

Used when the verb describes an inanimate subject, such as an inanimate noun being red.

VTA · Transitive animate

Used with an animate grammatical object, such as seeing a rock classified as animate.

VTI · Transitive inanimate

Used with an inanimate grammatical object, such as seeing a shoe.

Remember: these classes follow Cree grammar, not whether something seems alive in English. Different verbs also have their own forms and patterns.
