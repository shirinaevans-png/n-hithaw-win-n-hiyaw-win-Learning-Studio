Topic, Focus & Topicalization

See how sentence structure can organize information in conversation.

1. What is topicalization?

Topic refers to what the speaker is talking about. Focus highlights new or especially important information. Topicalization is a syntactic construction where a topic appears in a prominent position, often near the beginning of a sentence.

Topicalization, left dislocation and focus fronting are not interchangeable terms. In Cree, the position of a constituent interacts with discourse context, grammar and prosody.

3. How does this relate to Cree?

Plains Cree has relatively flexible ordering of overt noun phrases. Verb morphology, including person marking and direct/inverse relationships, provides important grammatical information; word order may also serve discourse functions. This does not mean that any arrangement is always acceptable or that every initial element is topicalized.

For Woods Cree (TH), consult the dialect-specific syntax research before treating a Plains Cree ordering as a Woodland Cree model. The relevant starting points in your research collection include Donna Starks’s work on Woods Cree discourse and syntax and Amy Dahlstrom’s Plains Cree Morphosyntax.
