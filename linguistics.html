01 / Grammar

The four verb classes

The class labels describe the verb's grammatical relationships, not whether its English translation mentions a person or object.

VAI

Verb, animate intransitive

One animate subject; no grammatical object.

mîciso

eat

Ratt, p. 4 (printed)
VII

Verb, inanimate intransitive

Inanimate subject; no grammatical object.

pipon

It is winter.

Ratt, p. 5 (printed)
VTI

Verb, transitive inanimate

Action directed toward an inanimate grammatical object. Subclasses matter.

otina

take it (VTI-1)

Ratt, p. 5 (printed)
VTA

Verb, transitive animate

Action directed toward an animate grammatical object.

otin

take someone

Ratt, p. 5 (printed)
Important: VAI, VII, VTI and VTA are conjugation classes. In some references you may see VTI-1 or other subdivisions. The examples in this lab use Ratt's Woodland Cree orthography. Changing a dialect label does not automatically convert a paradigm into Plains Cree.

05 / Reference

Language structure reference

Phonology and writing

Woodland Cree SRO uses the long vowels â, î, ô and the th consonant combination. A change in vowel length can distinguish forms. The vocabulary page contains a separate interactive Woodland syllabics chart.

Morphology

A morpheme is a minimal meaningful grammatical unit. A stem is the form to which inflection is attached. Cree verbs may encode information about subject, object, number, mood, direction, and other categories through morphology.

Further study

Topics planned for future, source-attested learning activities include noun animacy and plurals, transitive verb agreement, obviation, direct/inverse marking, and additional morpheme patterns. Those paradigms should not be generated by simply swapping endings across verb classes.

Source references

Solomon Ratt, mâci-nîhithawîwin: Beginning Woods Cree (2026): pp. 4–5 (verb labels and examples), pp. 13 and 15 (wîki forms). Refer to the book for complete inflectional paradigms.
