About the project

IED 501: Linguistics for Language Revitalization

Final Project · Digital language-learning prototype

Learning with language

This learning studio introduces vocabulary and writing systems through active recall, recognition, and repeated practice. The project distinguishes nîhithawîwin (Woodland Cree, TH dialect) from nêhiyawêwin (Plains Cree, Y dialect).

Roman orthography is shown for each record. Woodland Cree syllabics can be explored separately through the interactive chart. The learning activities include Woodland and Plains introductions and dictionary lookup, with select automated syllabic renderings labelled as candidates rather than certified spellings.

Sources and acknowledgements

The included vocabulary carries source-code references from the supplied research workbook. Selected Woodland Cree vocabulary has been compared against Solomon Ratt, mâci-nîhithawîwin: Beginning Woods Cree (2026), with references on word cards. The chart draws on Syllabics.net ↗ · Online Cree Dictionary's Woods Cree syllabics table.
